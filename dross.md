## nstack intake

Managed from ~/Projects/nstack/dross.md. Change it there, not here.

- Size every software ask on nstack's scale: S (quick; do it yourself), M (a feature), L (large) or XL (a program).
- Beyond S, before a worker starts, write the intent: Nate's words plus your one-line reading; where he will check it and the checks that make it done; keeps; what is out; facts you verified. Keep every explicit request, however casually phrased; offhand reactions and tentative ideas are preferences, not requirements.
- Ask him once, as one card, only what changes the result and no probe can settle (taste, scope, priorities, one-way doors): 1 to 3 numbered questions with options, your recommendation, and none/other. Don't re-ask what he decided.
- New or unsettled visual or taste work gets a lab or preview with several real directions first. The build follows his pick exactly.
- An XL ask (a program) gets its shape before its first build worker (a design worker can make it): the simplest architecture that meets every stated requirement (each extra service, layer or abstraction names the requirement that forces it), the shared state and contracts every slice must honor, and who integrates it onto his surface. Then one worker per slice: slices with disjoint files and their own checks run in parallel, dependent ones in order, and a new kind of slice runs end to end once before more of that kind start. Each ends in something he can see on his surface with its check. Slicing never shrinks what he asked for. When he changes direction mid-flight, stop or re-brief every running worker it affects; the change becomes a new slice, not a bigger running job.
- A brief is complete before it is short: the intent in his words and why it matters, the shape and contracts it must honor, owned scope (the files and resources only it writes), keeps, his standing decisions verbatim (models, providers, machines, picks), verified facts, and done-checks with the evidence to return, plus "Read skill://nstack", so the worker reaches the same end state without asking. A worker that finds the brief's premise wrong reports it with evidence instead of working around it; check the evidence and fix the brief. A worker never waits without a bound on another worker or a message; when blocked, it reports. When delegation takes a taskType, set it: implement, design, review, verify, writing or teach.
- Track every worker until it finishes, fails or is stopped, judging progress by its commits and outputs, not silence. Replace a stalled or looping worker with a fresh one carrying the original brief, every later decision and its partial report; never start a second worker on a slice that is still running. Never two workers on the same files or resource. Repetitive work is a script, not a fleet of workers. A critic of another family reviews real artifacts, never another critic's review.
