# nstack grill

A grill gets Nate and you to one understanding of the important things before anything is built. He calls for one when a wrong reading would cost more than a few rounds of questions; you never start one. For this conversation it replaces the one-batch rule: questions come in rounds until he confirms.

**Before the first round**, read what the topic touches: the code, the ledger, his earlier picks. Facts are your job: anything the code, tools, his data or a quick probe can answer, look up yourself and never ask. Draft the frame (`skill://nstack` › Frame); the questions are its gaps.

**Each round** asks the important decisions whose prerequisites are settled, about five at most, most important first, because deep into a long round people stop reading and take the defaults. Important means it changes what he gets: the outcome and how he will judge it, what must not change, what is out, data that is hard to change later, one-way doors, taste, and the name for a thing when you and he use different words. Reversible details are yours to decide and list as defaults at the close. A round is one plain message:

```
**1. <title>**: the question; where there are options, each with what it does to his real code or product and what it costs.
→ <your recommended answer, and why in a line>
```

Word each question so "yes" takes your recommendation, so he can answer a round in one line ("1 yes, 2 b, 3 no: …"). "Show me" on a question means bring back what settles it (a count from the code, a lookup, a rough preview on his screen) with the next round; take his other answers now. When an answer conflicts with a fact, a keep or an earlier answer, show the evidence and ask once in the next round. Each round's answers close some branches and open others; ask the next round from there.

**Close** when the important branches are settled; details stay yours, and so does anything he waves off. Send the frame back in his words (a Decided line for each answer, your defaults, the names you agreed) and wait for his yes. Record it at the top of the ledger when the work is bigger than one sitting; agreed names go into the code as written, with no glossary or spec files unless he asks. Build nothing before his yes beyond the throwaway probes a "show me" needs. After it, carry on with the task under nstack (a shape for L/XL work starts from the confirmed frame and does not re-ask it); with no task, stop.
