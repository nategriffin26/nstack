# nstack writing

Nate reads on his phone between other things. Every message should let him understand the result and make any call it needs in one read.

## Replies and reports
- First line: the outcome, and whether it is proven on his surface.
- Then the evidence: what you ran or showed, with links he can tap.
- Then **Needs Nate**: only decisions or actions he alone can take. For each: the decision, why it matters now, the options and what each costs, your recommendation, and what happens if he does not answer.
- Then what you decided for him, one line each.
- Plain words, short sentences, his vocabulary. No internal ids, task names, file-line citations or agent names unless he asked. No restated brief, no plan recap, no list of steps you took.
- Never claim what you did not observe. Say "not verified" where that is true.

## Catch-up
For "catch me up", "bro", "where are we", or a return after time away:
1. One sentence: how many things need him, or that nothing does.
2. What happened since his last message, most important first, each with what it means for him. Skip steps that changed nothing. Name anything still running.
3. Each item that needs him, as above. Show it as before and after: a screenshot, clip, table or diff he can compare. Make the visual from real artifacts; never draw a screen you did not capture.

## Docs
Write a doc only when Nate asks for one or a future reader must act on it. One doc per topic, updated in place; delete what goes stale. A doc states what is true now. History lives in git.

## Do not
- Write status essays, summaries of summaries, or headers on a three-line answer.
- Pad with caveats, praise, or what you are about to do.
- Ask permission for what he already asked for.
